Method

How a resumebecomes a score.

01 — Why

A single job post can draw hundreds of applications. Reading them by hand is slow, and the two-hundredth resume rarely gets the attention the fifth one did.

JobMatrix does the first pass. It reads every resume, compares each one to the job description with the same rules every time, and puts the closest matches on top. People still make the decision; they just start from a shorter list.

02 — Pipeline

File in,
score out.

Everything happens in memory. Files are deleted as soon as their text is extracted.

Extract

Read the text

PyPDF2 for PDFs, docx2txt for Word documents, plain read for TXT.

Clean

Strip the noise

Lowercase, split into words, drop common English stop words such as "the" and "and".

Vectorize

Weigh each word

The job and every resume become TF-IDF vectors, one weight per word.

Compare

Measure the angle

Cosine similarity between the job vector and each resume vector gives the score.

Rank

Keep the top five

Sorted by score, with the job's key terms each resume matches and misses.

03 — TF-IDF

How much does a word matter? It scores high when it appears often in one document but rarely across the others. "Kubernetes" in a DevOps role carries far more weight than "team".

tf-idf(t, d) = tf(t, d) × log Ndf(t)
tf(t, d)
How often term t appears in document d
N
Total documents: the job plus every resume
df(t)
How many documents contain term t
04 — Cosine similarity

How close are two documents? Treat each as an arrow in word-space. The smaller the angle between the job and a resume, the better the fit.

cos θ = A · B‖A‖ ‖B‖
A · B
Dot product of the job and resume vectors
‖A‖ ‖B‖
The length of each vector
0 to 1
Nothing in common, to identical wording
05 — Limits

What it does,
and what it doesn't.

Good at

  • Fast, consistent first-pass screening of large batches
  • Finding resumes that cover the role's specific tools and skills
  • Scores you can explain, keyword by keyword

Not yet

  • Synonyms: "ML" and "machine learning" count as different words
  • Scanned, image-only PDFs
  • Judging seniority or quality, only word overlap
  • Next: semantic matching with sentence embeddings
